Opening

Fast answers for the things that social media says.

Question 01

How do I know if something is legit?

Ask two things. What's the actual mechanism, not just what it claims to do. And is it clinically dosed? If either answer is not clear, it's marketing. Do the research yourself. Get on google, ChatGPT, or whatever method you want.

If it is a supplement; ask if it is clinically dosed, meaning the dose provided is in an amount that is actually effective to what your body needs. Ask if you can get this from your diet and if actually taking this supplement is meaningful to your goals. Also, is it something you are actually deficient in, most times a blood test is the only way to tell if you are deficient. This all leads back to having a conversation with your doctor.

Question 02

Do keto, intermittent fasting, paleo, or cleanses work?

They work the same way every diet works: fewer calories. Keto drops water fast due to the decrease in carbs. Fasting just shrinks your eating window. Paleo cuts junk. Cleanses are laxatives. None of them beat a calorie deficit, they just hide one inside a set of rules.

Every diet with a name is marketing. THE ONLY THING THEY ALL SHARE IS THE THING THEY DON'T ADVERTISE, WHICH IS FEWER CALORIES.

If one of these is genuinely how you like to eat, keep it. I'll build your numbers around it.

Question 03

Can I just exercise instead of dieting?

No. I would rather see you track your food and not go to the gym. A hard 30 minute workout burns about 200 to 300 calories, roughly one bagel. Train for strength and health. Don't train to cancel out food.

You are not in the gym for 23 hours each day. Use those hours wisely.

Question 04

Does stress or cortisol stop me from losing fat?

No. Cortisol can cause water retention, which hides real fat loss on the scale for a few days. It does not turn off fat loss. The actual link between stress and weight gain is behavior: worse sleep, more food, fewer steps. Even in real medical cases of chronically high cortisol, the fat gain comes from more appetite and fat getting stored around the belly, not from fat burning shutting off.

Question 05

Is my metabolism broken? What's starvation mode?

Almost never. Your metabolism does adapt as you lose weight, but only by around 5 to 15 percent below what a calculator predicts. Real, but nowhere near enough to stall you. If the scale is actually stuck, it's usually unlogged food, water weight, or a deficit that's gone stale and needs recalculating.

If your weight stalls for two weeks, where your food intake is perfectly logged, you are drinking plenty of water, you are having regular bowel movements, and you are hitting your step goal, then yes your metabolism has caught up to your intake. This is when we adjust. After one week of stalled weight with perfect execution of the Base Habits, I would likely tell you to add a little more steps and drink more water. But we need to see true consistency to call it a stall. This is where you really need to be honest with yourself about how consistent and dialed in you are with your plan.

Question 06

Do fat burners or supplements work?

Mostly no. Fat burners are just caffeine. Detox teas are laxatives. Spot reduction isn't real. Some vitamins are worth taking if you're actually deficient. Get a blood test before you buy anything.

The only supplements I personally take are creatine, vitamin D, vitamin B, Magnesium before bed, and zinc in the morning. Plus a hydration supplement, such as Liquid IV. I actually make my own hydration supplement, because it is cheaper. I get my blood tested at least once or twice a year and I feel confident that I am not deficient in anything else based on my tests.

Question 07

What's actually true, then?

  • A calorie deficit is required for fat loss. Calories in vs calories out.
  • Protein protects your muscles while you lose fat.
  • Steps move your daily burn more than a workout does.
  • Sleep affects your appetite and your decisions the next day.
  • Consistency across months beats a couple high intensity weeks here and there.
Question 08

How do I spot a bad fitness post?

Ask:

01

Is a product being sold to me?

Marketing has taken over social media as well as regular TV programs and commercials. The whole reason I got into coaching was because I got sick and tired of all the exaggerated information out there that people decided they can market and sell to people who do not know any better.

02

Is it clinically dosed and am I deficient in it?

Do a blood test and your own research.

03

Does it name a single enemy food or holy grail food?

No single food or food group will make you fat or cause fat loss. It is 100% based on your calorie deficit.

04

Does it promise speed or give an exact timeline of when results will be seen?

Actual fat loss rates are different for everyone and it is directly tied to how much of a calorie deficit you are in and for how long.

05

Are the ingredients listed or does it say "proprietary blend"

Proprietary blend is a dead giveaway that they are hiding the exact ingredients in the product.

Send me anything new before you try it. I will gladly research it for you. I also will point you to your doctor if I am unsure or think it is worth their opinion.
Closing

It is best to be skeptical when scrolling or watching anything. Looking at the nutrition label and a quick AI search is far better than whatever is on the front of the box. The moral of this page is to do your own research and talk to your doctor.
