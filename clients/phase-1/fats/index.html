Fats

Client Home › Base Habits › Two Week Rule › Debunking › Calories › Macros › Fats
Opening

Fat is not bad for you. It’s just calorie dense.

Nine calories per gram, more than double protein or carbs. You’re not cutting it out. You’re watching how much of it you eat, because your calories only stretch so far, and fat is the least filling macro of the three.

Section 1

What Fat Does (other than make food taste amazing)

Cutting fat too low causes problems that have nothing to do with losing weight.

That’s why fat gets a number to hit, not a ceiling to stay under.

01

Hormone production

Your body uses fat to produce hormones, including the ones tied to strength, recovery, and menstrual cycle. Go too low for too long and this is usually the first thing to break. For women that often shows up as a missed or irregular period.

02

Vitamin absorption

Vitamins A, D, E, and K dissolve in fat, not water. No fat in the meal, and they pass straight through you. Eat them with something that has fat in it.

03

Cell function

Every cell in your body has a membrane built partly from fat. Hitting your number covers it. More fat doesn’t build better cells.

04

The taste factor

Fat carries flavor. The oil, the butter, the dressing, the sauce, that’s most of what makes a meal taste like something you’d eat again. It also slows digestion, so food moves through you slower.

It still won’t fill you up. That’s protein’s job. Fat makes the meal taste good, but that’s something to stay skeptical of.

Section 2

The Number

You get a fat number and you stay as close to it as you can.

Fat works like protein. You get a number and you hit it.

I set it with the rest of your numbers. Go above it and every extra gram is calories pulled from protein or carbs, the two macros doing more of the actual work right now. Carbs are your energy. Protein protects your muscle.

You won’t land on it exactly. Get close, within 5 grams is a goal I stick to.

There’s no prize for going lower. Under your number you feel worse, recover worse, and gain nothing.
Section 3

The Types, Briefly

The type of fat matters far less than most people have been told, and far less than the amount. Your goal is to eat mostly whole foods, and whole foods answer the type question for you.

You’ve heard this framed as good fats and bad fats. It’s not that clean.

Unsaturated fat. Olive oil, avocado, nuts, seeds, fatty fish. Most consistently linked to long term heart health. Make most of your fat come from here.

Saturated fat. Butter, fatty cuts of meat, full fat dairy, coconut oil. It spent decades as the villain, and the evidence turned out weaker than the headlines. Not something to fear, not a free pass either. Most health organizations still recommend keeping it the smaller share. Reasonable default.

Trans fat. The one real exception. Genuine harm, and it’s mostly been regulated out of the food supply. See partially hydrogenated oil on an ingredient list, skip it.

One line on the medical side. If your doctor has given you a specific plan for cholesterol, blood pressure, or heart health, follow it. That’s their call, not mine.

Every gram of fat is nine calories, no matter the source. Olive oil and butter cost you the same.

Section 4

What to Eat

You are not chasing fat. You are deciding where it comes from.

Fat mostly arrives attached to other food, so this decision gets made at the store, not at the table. Buy whole foods and most of it sorts itself out.

01

Worth having in the house

  • Olive oil for cooking and for dressing
  • Whole eggs
  • Salmon, sardines, and mackerel
  • Avocado
  • Nuts and seeds, portioned into a bowl before you eat them, never eaten out of the bag
  • Nut butter with two ingredients on the list
  • Full fat dairy if you like it and it fits, in an amount you measured
02

Fat you get without choosing it

  • What’s already in your beef, pork, chicken thighs, eggs, and cheese
  • Whatever’s in the sauce, the dressing, the marinade, and the pan
Control your fat at the store, not the table. Buy protein lean and add the fat you actually want on purpose, instead of buying it already mixed in.
Section 5

Where Fat Hides

Fat is easy to overeat because most of it doesn’t look like food.

Nobody overdoes it on a steak. They overdo it on the things around the steak.

Two tablespoons of oil in a pan carry about the same calories as a chicken breast, and only one of them ends up on the plate where you can see it.

The fix isn’t cutting it out BY THE GRAM. It’s measuring what you pour. Oil, dressing, nut butter, cream, people eyeball these and they’re usually off by double. MEASURE WHAT YOU POUR.

01

Cooking for a table

You’re cooking one dinner for the house, so the oil goes in before anyone has a plate. Measure it into the pan, then split it across however many servings the pan makes. That’s the only honest way to log a meal you didn’t cook just for yourself.

Or skip the math. Cook the protein plain, then let everyone add their own butter, ranch, and cheese at the table. Everyone gets what they want, and you know exactly what’s in yours.

Section 6

How to Read a Label

Fat is where the front of the package works hardest to distract you.

01

Serving size, then servings per container

Nothing else on the nutrition label means anything until you know the serving size. Nut butter is the classic trap. The serving is two tablespoons, and eyeballing it usually gets you closer to three. Weigh it in grams instead of guessing with a spoon.

02

Total fat, then calories

Total fat is what goes in your tracker. Saturated and trans are listed underneath as a breakdown of it, not on top of it.

03

Run the 10x rule while you’re there

Cross out the last digit of the calorie number and compare what’s left to the grams of protein. Ten or under and the food is a real protein source. Over fifteen and it’s a fat or a carb with a little protein attached, no matter what the front of the package says.

Almonds are 165 calories and 6 grams of protein. Cross out the 5 and you have 16 against 6 grams. Not close. Peanut butter comes out the same way. Both are fats, and both get filed in the protein column in people’s heads. Protein

04

Zero grams of trans fat doesn’t always mean zero

A product can print 0 g of trans fat when a serving has up to half a gram in it. Eat three servings and you’ve had more than a gram of the one fat genuinely worth avoiding. The tell is the ingredient list. See partially hydrogenated oil, put it back.

05

Cooking spray is not zero calories

The serving size is a spray of about a quarter of a second, which is how it rounds down to zero. Nobody sprays for a quarter of a second. If you use it, log a teaspoon of oil for every three or four seconds of spraying and stop thinking about it.

06

Fat free and light usually means sugar

When fat comes out, sugar, starch, or gums go in to make it taste like food again. Check the calories. Sometimes the fat free version isn’t actually lower in calories.

07

Made with olive oil is not made of olive oil

It means there’s some in there. The ingredient list runs in order by weight, and it’ll tell you where the olive oil actually ranks, which is often behind one or two other oils.

Section 7

Where People Go Wrong

The mistakes here run in both directions.

Treating healthy fats as free. Avocado, olive oil, almonds. Great foods, same nine calories per gram as everything else. Eat them on purpose, in a portion you picked before opening the bag.

Cooking without measuring. The single biggest untracked source of calories most people have. A generous pour is three tablespoons easy. That’s 360 calories you didn’t log.

Eating off your kids’ plates. Nuggets, mac and cheese, buttered noodles, the crust with butter on it, the last few fries. Kid food is built out of fat and refined carbs, which makes it the most calorie dense food in your house, and none of it feels like a meal when you eat it standing at the counter.

Cutting it to nothing. Fat free everything, dry salads, no oil at all. Works for about three weeks, then stops, because it’s miserable and you feel worse. Two Week Rule

Believing certain fats burn fat. Coconut oil, MCTs, whatever’s going around this month. None of them are calorie free and none of them override your total calories in and calories out. Debunking

Closing

What to Do With This

Fat isn’t the enemy here, and it isn’t the lever either. It’s just the most calorie dense thing on your plate. Eat enough of it, then stop.

Hit your number. Buy your protein lean. Keep your food whole. Measure what you pour. Put what you save into the two macros doing more of the actual work.
